Abstract—
Customer defection is one of the most financially damaging operational challenges in the telecommunications industry, where replacing a departing subscriber costs five to seven times more than retaining an existing one. Conventional churn pipelines often apply a fixed τ = 0.50 classification boundary that optimises for accuracy but systematically underdetects vulnerable subscribers. This paper presents an end-to-end explainable ensemble learning and operational retention decision-support system evaluated on balanced_telco_churn.csv (N = 8,000 records; exactly 50% churners). Three tree-based base classifiers —Random Forest, Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM)—are trained independently on SMOTE-augmented data and fused through an out-of-fold Stacking Ensemble governed by a Logistic Regression meta-learner. A domain-informed feature engineering pipeline expands the original 21 raw attributes into 83 model-ready features. Per-model decision thresholds are systematically optimised: XGBoost achieves 97.6% recall, 94.9% accuracy, and 99.0% ROC-AUC at τ = 0.29; LightGBM attains 96.9% recall, 95.0% accuracy, and 98.9% ROC-AUC at τ = 0.32; Random Forest records 96.3% recall, 95.4% accuracy, and 98.7% ROC-AUC at τ = 0.38; and the Stacking Ensemble delivers 96.3% recall, 95.4% accuracy, 95.5% F1-score, 94.7% precision, 98.9% ROC-AUC, and a Brier score of 0.0362 at τ = 0.26. A dual SHAP and LIME explainability pipeline feeds a state-aware retention recommendation engine mapping model evidence to prioritised business interventions. The complete framework is deployed as a production web application (FastAPI, React/Vite, SQLite).
Index Terms—Customer Churn Prediction, Telecom Analytics, Stacking Ensemble Learning, Decision Threshold Optimization, Explainable AI, SHAP, LIME, Retention Recommendations

I. Introduction

The rapid expansion of mobile broadband, fibre-optic internet, and bundled digital services has transformed telecommunications into one of the most intensely competitive sectors globally. Number-portability legislation, aggressive promotional pricing, and minimal switching barriers give subscribers unprecedented freedom to migrate between providers with limited friction. As a result, operators routinely face annual subscriber attrition rates exceeding 20–30%, and monthly churn rates in mature markets commonly sit between 1.5% and 3.0% [1].

The economic asymmetry between retention and acquisition makes churn prediction strategically critical. Acquiring a replacement subscriber can cost five to seven times more than retaining an existing one once sales commissions, device subsidies, onboarding support, and margin loss during the re-acquisition cycle are accounted for [2]. High-value subscribers with premium plans, multiple services, and long tenure represent a disproportionate share of operator profitability.

Manual identification of at-risk accounts through rule-based heuristics is fundamentally inadequate at scale. Subscriber behaviour emerges from a complex interplay of contract type, billing level, payment modality, service portfolio, and tenure trajectory that static rules cannot reliably parse [3]. A subscriber paying above-average monthly charges on a month-to-month contract via electronic check, for example, carries a compound risk profile from three interacting attributes, none of which is individually decisive.

Machine learning addresses this challenge by modelling multi-feature interactions without requiring explicit rule specification. Tree-based ensembles—particularly gradient-boosted frameworks—have delivered consistently strong performance on tabular CRM data [4]. Despite these advances, most deployed systems apply the default 0.50 probability threshold, which is sub-optimal when false negatives (missed churners) carry substantially higher cost than false positives [5]. Furthermore, even highly accurate models remain actionability-blind: they produce a probability label but no guidance on why a customer is at risk or which retention intervention is most effective [6].

This work addresses these gaps through: (i) domain-driven feature engineering expanding 21 raw attributes to 83 features; (ii) three independently configured tree classifiers combined through an out-of-fold stacking ensemble; (iii) per-model threshold optimisation; (iv) a dual SHAP and LIME explainability pipeline; and (v) a state-aware retention recommendation engine.

II. Literature Review

Churn prediction has attracted sustained research across machine learning and telecommunications management. Early studies relied on logistic regression and decision trees [7], establishing the relevance of contract type, tenure, and monthly charges—features that continue to dominate modern importance rankings.

Ensemble learning demonstrated substantial improvements over individual classifiers. Hadden et al. [8] showed that bagging multiple decision trees significantly stabilised accuracy. Verbeke et al. [9] confirmed that ensembles consistently outperform single classifiers, particularly under class imbalance.

Gradient-boosted architectures—XGBoost [10] and LightGBM [11]— introduced more powerful optimisation frameworks. Farquad et al. [12] demonstrated that gradient-boosted trees capture non-obvious billing and contract interactions that simpler models miss. Chawla et al. [13] introduced SMOTE for class rebalancing; subsequent work established that fold-isolated SMOTE prevents target leakage [14].

SHAP [15] and LIME [16] provide global and local feature interpretability for complex models. Stacking-based generalisation [18] trains a meta-learner on out-of-fold base-classifier predictions, capturing complementary strengths while avoiding overfitting. Provost and Fawcett [20] established ROC analysis as the principled basis for threshold selection under asymmetric misclassification costs.

The research gap is the integration of all these components—ensemble learning, fold-isolated SMOTE, threshold optimisation, dual explainability, and retention automation—into a coherent end-to-end deployed production system.

III. Proposed Framework Overview

Fig. 1. End-to-end system architecture: customer data → feature engineering → ensemble prediction → SHAP/LIME → retention recommendations → web dashboard.

The developed framework processes subscriber records through a seven-stage pipeline:

  1. Data Ingestion: Subscriber attributes received via single-customer form or CSV batch upload.
  2. Feature Engineering: Backend constructs 83-feature vector from six core inputs automatically.
  3. Base Model Prediction: RF, XGBoost, and LightGBM independently estimate churn probability.
  4. Ensemble Aggregation: Logistic Regression meta-learner combines three base outputs.
  5. Threshold Decision: Each model applies its optimised τ; probabilities ≥ τ trigger churn-risk flags.
  6. Explainability: SHAP TreeExplainer and LIME surrogate compute per-feature attributions.
  7. Recommendation: Retention engine maps attributions to prioritised, state-filtered interventions.

VI. Model Development

A. Random Forest

A Random Forest [22] builds an ensemble of T independent decision trees, each trained on a bootstrap sample with a random feature subset at each split node. Churn probability for subscriber x:

PRF(x) = (1/T) Σt=1T ht(x)(1)

Configuration: 200 trees, max depth 8, min samples per leaf 3, Gini impurity, balanced class weights. At τ = 0.38: Recall 96.3%, Accuracy 95.4%, ROC-AUC 98.7%.

B. XGBoost

XGBoost [10] trains trees sequentially to correct residuals, minimising the regularised objective:

L(k) = Σi l(yi, ŷi(k-1) + hk(xi)) + Ω(hk)(2)

where Ω(hk) = γT + λ||w||²/2. Configuration: 150 trees, lr 0.04, max depth 3, λ = 2.0, α = 0.2. At τ = 0.29: Recall 97.6%, Accuracy 94.9%, ROC-AUC 99.0%, Brier 0.0347—highest recall and ROC-AUC of all evaluated models.

C. LightGBM

LightGBM [11] employs leaf-wise growth, splitting the leaf with the highest loss gain. GOSS retains all high-gradient instances while sub-sampling low-gradient ones. Configuration: 120 trees, lr 0.04, 24 leaves, max depth 3, L2 reg 2.5. At τ = 0.32: Recall 96.9%, Accuracy 95.0%, ROC-AUC 98.9%, Brier 0.0353.

D. Stacking Ensemble

The stacking architecture fuses three base classifiers through a Logistic Regression meta-learner trained on out-of-fold (OOF) predictions from 5-fold stratified cross-validation. The meta-feature matrix:

M = [pRF | pXGB | pLGBM] ∈ ℝn×3(3)
PEns(x) = σ(w0 + w1pRF + w2pXGB + w3pLGBM)(4)
Fig. 2. Stacking ensemble: base classifiers generate OOF probability vectors assembled into the meta-feature matrix; Logistic Regression meta-learner produces final churn probability.

At τ = 0.26: Recall 96.3%, F1 95.5%, Accuracy 95.4%, Precision 94.7%, ROC-AUC 98.9%, Brier 0.0362.

IX. Results Analysis

All four classifiers achieve ROC-AUC between 98.7% and 99.0%, indicating near-perfect rank separation between churners and non-churners.

XGBoost records the highest recall (97.6%) and ROC-AUC (99.0%). It correctly identifies 97.6% of actual churners, leaving only 2.4% undetected. The Brier score of 0.0347 is also the lowest, suggesting the best-calibrated probability outputs. The trade-off is precision of 92.5%, reflecting the false-positive cost of the aggressive τ = 0.29 threshold.

LightGBM achieves 96.9% recall, the highest accuracy (95.0%), and a Brier score of 0.0353. Its leaf-wise growth strategy produces a favourable balance between false-negative reduction and false-positive control.

Random Forest achieves 96.3% recall and 95.4% accuracy with the most conservative threshold (0.38). Its precision of 94.6% is the second highest.

Stacking Ensemble achieves the highest F1-score (95.5%) and precision (94.7%), with ROC-AUC 98.9% and Brier 0.0362. The meta-learner successfully reduces the false-positive rate relative to individual gradient-boosted models, producing a more stable profile for operational deployment.

XI. Explainable AI & Retention Recommendations

A. SHAP Feature Attribution

For subscriber i with p features, SHAP [15] satisfies:

f(x(i)) = φ0 + Σj=1p φj(i)(11)

Features with positive φj push toward churn; negative values push toward retention. The system uses SHAP TreeExplainer (exact for tree-based models). Global rankings are obtained by averaging |φj(i)| across the dataset.

B. LIME Explanation Service

LIME [16] approximates the ensemble with a sparse linear surrogate near each prediction:

ξ(x0) = argming L(f, g, πx0) + Ω(g)(12)

C. Retention Recommendation Engine

Fig. 4. Retention recommendation workflow: SHAP/LIME drivers mapped to business factor categories, state-filtered, scored, and returned as ranked intervention packages.

The engine operates in four stages: (1) Risk Classification (Low/Medium/High/Critical); (2) Driver Extraction (SHAP+LIME → business factor categories); (3) State-Aware Filtering (prune inapplicable interventions); (4) Prioritised Ranking by composite score. Interventions include: contract upgrade incentives, automatic payment migration, billing discount review, onboarding support, service bundles, technical support, and fibre quality investigation.

D. What-If Simulator & Batch Prediction

The What-If Simulator allows analysts to modify customer attributes and observe how the ensemble probability responds, without asserting causal effects. Batch Upload enables CSV-based multi-customer prediction with downloadable results including probability, risk level, and primary SHAP driver.

XII. Discussion

The consistently strong performance of all three tree-based classifiers reflects the suitability of gradient-boosted and bagged ensembles for tabular CRM data. The stacking ensemble's value lies in robustness: achieving the highest F1 and precision while matching other models on recall indicates the meta-learner successfully identifies regions where each base classifier is most reliable.

Decision threshold optimisation proved essential. Operating all models at 0.50 would produce substantially lower recall—a trade-off that is operationally sub-optimal when the cost of missing a churner exceeds unnecessary outreach cost.

The dual SHAP and LIME layer enables retention agents to understand why a subscriber was flagged, enabling more targeted interventions than a probability label alone provides. Results apply to the balanced benchmark and require recalibration for real-world 15–30% churn prevalence.

XIII. Limitations

The 50/50 class distribution is artificial; production deployments would alter thresholds and false-positive rates. All evaluation uses a single dataset; external validation is needed. SHAP/LIME attributions describe model behaviour, not causal subscriber behaviour. Retention recommendations are heuristic and untested in controlled field trials.

XIV. Conclusion

This work developed an end-to-end explainable ensemble learning framework for telecom customer churn prediction. Starting from 8,000 balanced subscriber records, feature engineering expanded 21 raw attributes to 83 model-ready features. Three tree classifiers were combined through an out-of-fold Stacking Ensemble. Per-model thresholds were optimised: XGBoost achieved 97.6% recall and 99.0% ROC-AUC; LightGBM recorded 96.9% recall and 98.9% ROC-AUC; Random Forest delivered 96.3% recall and 98.7% ROC-AUC; and the Stacking Ensemble produced 96.3% recall, 95.5% F1, 94.7% precision, 98.9% ROC-AUC, and Brier 0.0362.

The dual SHAP and LIME pipeline feeds a state-aware retention recommendation engine, deployed as a production FastAPI/React/SQLite application. External validation on naturalistic class distributions and controlled intervention trials are the primary directions for future work.

XV. Future Enhancement

Future work will explore: production deployment validation; isotonic regression calibration for natural 15–30% churn prevalence; longitudinal dataset expansion with time-series usage features; uplift modeling for individual treatment effect estimation; customer lifetime value integration; causal inference for structural driver identification; concept drift monitoring with automated retraining; online learning for near-real-time model refinement; and closed-loop intervention effectiveness tracking.
