Abstract— Customer churn poses substantial financial risk to the telecommunications industry, where acquiring replacement subscribers incurs five to seven times the cost of retaining existing accounts. Standard machine learning implementations frequently employ symmetric decision thresholds (τ=0.50) that yield severe false-negative rates, failing to flag vulnerable subscribers prior to cancellation. In this work, we present an end-to-end explainable ensemble learning and operational retention decision support framework evaluated on the domain-consistent balanced_telco_churn.csv benchmark dataset (N=8,000, exactly 50% churners and 50% loyal accounts). Our methodology evaluates Random Forest, Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), and a Stacking Ensemble governed by an out-of-fold cross-validated Logistic Regression meta-learner. Through systematic classification threshold optimization, our models achieve high operational sensitivity without sacrificing discriminatory precision: XGBoost achieves 97.6% recall, 94.9% accuracy, and 99.0% ROC-AUC at τ=0.29; LightGBM attains 96.9% recall, 95.0% accuracy, and 98.9% ROC-AUC at τ=0.32; Random Forest records 96.3% recall, 95.4% accuracy, and 98.7% ROC-AUC at τ=0.38; and the Stacking Ensemble yields 96.3% recall, 95.4% accuracy, 95.5% F1-score, 94.7% precision, 98.9% ROC-AUC, and a Brier calibration score of 0.0362 at τ=0.26. To bridge the gap between statistical probability estimates and operational retention management, we integrate a dual explainability pipeline combining Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME). These interpretability vectors feed a state-aware retention recommendation engine that prunes inapplicable interventions and ranks cost-effective retention packages. The framework is deployed across a production full-stack architecture featuring a FastAPI backend, React 19 interface, SQLite database, and an interactive counterfactual What-If simulator.
Keywords—Customer Churn Prediction, Stacking Ensemble Generalization, Decision Threshold Optimization, Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), SHAP, LIME, Telecom Retention Analytics.

I. Introduction

The use of telecommunications and high-speed digital connectivity has entirely transformed how modern enterprises interact, communicate, exchange information, and manage business workflows. Saturated telecommunications markets characterized by extensive carrier infrastructure, number portability legislation, and aggressive competitor promotions provide unprecedented consumer choice. However, service providers experience average monthly subscriber churn rates ranging between 1.5% and 3.0%, compounding to annual subscriber attrition exceeding 20% to 30% [1]. Customer defection can be defined as the intentional termination of service by subscribers who transition to alternative providers, representing an existential challenge that directly impairs profitability and enterprise valuation [2].

The dynamic and multifaceted character of customer billing, service usage, and customer support interactions renders traditional manual filtering and rule-based heuristic checks obsolete [3]. Subscriber relationships are governed by a complex interplay of contract terms, monthly fees, unexpected price inflation, network stability, and payment friction. Indeed, a single billing variance might not prompt an immediate cancellation, but when paired with month-to-month contracts and electronic check payments, it triggers high churn probability. These attributes necessitate advanced computer models that can process multi-feature interactions, lifecycle nuances, and non-linear patterns in combination [4].

Customer churn forecasting has significantly improved over the past few years due to artificial intelligence (AI), in particular, ensemble machine learning and tabular deep architectures [5]. Classical algorithms such as logistic regression and single decision trees demonstrated limited capacity to separate intricate boundary cases. In contrast, advanced ensemble models including Random Forests, Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), and heterogeneous Stacking Ensembles have established state-of-the-art predictive performance across customer relationship management benchmarks [6].

However, despite these computational advances, contemporary enterprise churn pipelines remain vulnerable to four critical systemic issues: (1) Asymmetric misclassification costs where false negatives inflict catastrophic recurring revenue loss while false positives incur trivial check-in costs; (2) Metric imbalance where naive optimizations improve precision at the expense of capturing defection; (3) Suboptimal reliance on default symmetric 0.50 classification cutoffs; and (4) The black-box dilemma where complex gradient-boosted ensembles fail to provide operational reasoning for retention agents [7].

An idea that is suggested as a solution to these issues is the concept of an integrated, explainable stacking ensemble framework coupled with decision threshold optimization and state-aware recommendation workflows [8]. Multimodal analytics better represent the true customer lifecycle because they synthesize core billing parameters, non-linear tenure dynamics, interaction terms, and localized explainability vectors. Besides improving classification accuracy, this integration simplifies the deployment of real-time retention decision support systems across web and mobile platforms [9].

This work proposes an end-to-end explainable ensemble learning architecture combining Random Forest for variance suppression, XGBoost and LightGBM for gradient-boosted decision boundary refinement, and a regularized meta-learner trained strictly on out-of-fold cross-validated probability vectors [10]. To eliminate the false-negative penalty, decision thresholds are systematically tuned across cross-validation folds. The system integrates SHAP and LIME dual explainability to generate verified feature importance and powers an automated retention recommendation engine. The result is an accurate, transparent, and actionable customer churn prevention framework [11].

II. Literature Review

To identify customer defection risks, prior studies have investigated statistical methodologies and machine learning pipelines, evaluating such factors as customer tenure, pricing elasticity, contract structures, and service bundles. Approaches based on tree ensembles, gradient boosting, and hybrid stacking architectures have proven highly promising in establishing predictive accuracy and operational stability.

The authors [12] presented a machine learning system for detecting churn on telecommunication customer datasets. They demonstrated that non-linear ensemble architectures capture complex multi-feature interactions significantly more effectively than classical linear classifiers.

The authors [13] recommended the use of Explainable AI (XAI) to interpret tree ensembles for subscriber churn identification. In their work, the challenge of interpreting complex decision trees was addressed through additive feature attributions, enabling business specialists to diagnose the root causes of defection.

The researchers [14] investigated customer sentiment and churn classification using supervised learning pipelines. Their findings highlighted the necessity of integrating service interaction history with financial parameters to optimize retention interventions.

The authors [15] examined sentence-level customer feedback and tabular churn records using Python and machine learning. Their research identified the advantages of hybrid modeling to track customer dissatisfaction across multi-channel communication touchpoints.

The researchers [16] employed gradient boosting architectures to investigate subscriber loyalty patterns. They demonstrated that customer churn is disproportionately concentrated in early lifecycle stages and uncommitted contract categories.

The detectors of customer churn developed in [17] optimized tree ensemble hyperparameters through Bayesian optimization. To cope with high-dimensional feature interactions, their study emphasized the importance of rigorous cross-validation and feature transformation.

The authors [18] proposed an ensemble churn classification framework integrating Support Vector Machines with Random Forests. As their study confirmed, ensemble combinations identify subtle defection indicators more reliably than individual models.

In [19], the authors provided an analytical review of tabular machine learning methods for customer retention. The methods examined included gradient-based decision trees, neural network surrogates, and cost-sensitive loss formulations, emphasizing the need for operational fairness and interpretability.

The research article [20] investigated class balancing strategies on augmented telecommunications records, confirming that balanced data structures significantly enhance sensitivity toward defection events.

The dataset provides complete account records, billing histories, and service subscriptions across 8,000 verified customer profiles (Table I).

Data cleaning is an essential aspect of preprocessing. Non-numeric entries in TotalCharges are coerced to valid floats, missing values are imputed via median strategy, and numerical predictors are scaled using StandardScaler. Category columns undergo one-hot dummy encoding.

Real-time heuristics dynamically evaluate business rules during inference. The proposed system flow is depicted in Fig. 1. Customer account ingestion, feature engineering, ensemble classification, threshold sweeping, dual XAI attribution, and retention action generation operate as an integrated pipeline.

Fig. 1. Architecture Diagram of Proposed Churn Prediction Framework

Random Forest encodes decorrelated tree votes, XGBoost captures second-order gradient residuals, and LightGBM applies leaf-wise tree growth. The out-of-fold probability vectors are combined by the stacking meta-learner into an ensemble risk score.

The fraction of correctly reported accounts, both churn and loyal, compared to total accounts is called Accuracy in Equation 1. It provides an overall indicator of system efficacy.

Accuracy = (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN)
(1)

Precision in Equation 2 is the ratio of correctly predicted churners to total predicted churners, minimizing false positive interventions.

Precision = TP / (TP + FP)
(2)

Recall in Equation 3 determines the percentage of correctly identified churners out of all actual churners, directly protecting subscriber revenue.

Recall = TP / (TP + FN)
(3)

F1-score in Equation 4 achieves the optimal harmonic balance between Recall and Precision in operational environments.

F1-Score = 2 × (Precision × Recall) / (Precision + Recall)
(4)

There are multiple ensemble learning models evaluated in this research. Primary metrics including Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and ROC-AUC determine the classification excellence of each algorithm.

Fig. 2. Evaluation Metrics Values for Random Forest Model

Random Forest was evaluated by its ability to classify test instances using standard criteria. The model achieved 95.4% accuracy, 94.6% precision, 96.3% recall, and 95.4% F1-score (see Fig. 2). Random Forest was trained using 250 trees with maximum depth of 12.

Fig. 3. Evaluation Metrics Values for Gradient Boosted Models (XGBoost & LightGBM)

Gradient boosted architectures provide exceptional sensitivity. XGBoost achieved 97.6% recall with 94.9% accuracy at τ*=0.29, leaving only 19 defection false negatives out of 800 actual churners. LightGBM attained 96.9% recall, 95.0% accuracy, and 98.9% ROC-AUC at τ*=0.32 (see Fig. 3).

Fig. 4. Evaluation Metrics Values for Proposed Stacking Model

The proposed Stacking Ensemble combines Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM via an out-of-fold Logistic Regression meta-learner. As shown in Fig. 4, it achieved 95.4% accuracy, 94.7% precision, 96.3% recall, 95.5% F1-score, and 98.9% ROC-AUC at τ*=0.26.

Fig. 5. Working Flow Diagram of Decision Support System

The working flow diagram of the end-to-end decision support framework is shown in Fig. 5. Incoming customer accounts undergo feature extraction, ensemble probability scoring, optimized thresholding, dual-XAI attribution, and automated business intervention ranking.
